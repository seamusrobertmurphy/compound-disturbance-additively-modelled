# Refitting a beetle by fire disturbance interaction

*Why the additive model of beetle mortality and burn severity was the defensible one, Darkwoods Conservation Area, British Columbia*

## Abstract

Where a mountain pine beetle outbreak precedes wildfire, the outbreak may change the severity of the fire, a linked disturbance, or change what the fire leaves behind, a compound disturbance, and field studies of lodgepole pine at short intervals have found little of either. Few studies have tested both on one landscape with the same surfaces, and fewer have asked whether the likelihood used to fit a beetle by fire product term in a regeneration model can be trusted to judge it. This study tested both on the beetle outbreak of 2005 to 2014 and the mixed-severity Mt Midgeley wildfire of 2015 in the Darkwoods Conservation Area, southern Selkirk Mountains, British Columbia (annual Landsat beetle classifications, a classified burn severity raster, the provincial Aerial Overview Survey, and the mapped conifer seedlings of three four-hectare plots). The probability of high-severity burning was modelled on prior grey-stage attack, lodgepole pine cover and elevation by logistic regression with spatial block bootstrap intervals, and the parent study's inhomogeneous point process model of regeneration was refitted with beetle by fire product terms under its published Poisson likelihood, under a spatial block bootstrap and under a cluster-robust sandwich estimator. Prior attack did not change the probability of high-severity burning (log-odds 0.043 for attacked against unattacked cells, block bootstrap interval spanning zero at every block size), and the count of attack years showed no hump-shaped effect. The product terms were decisive under the Poisson likelihood (likelihood ratio test, p < 0.001) and absent under inference that respected the clustering the parent study documented; the coefficients were identical, the intervals 2.3 to 5.9 times wider, none of sixteen product-term intervals excluded zero, and the sandwich estimator agreed. Neither a linked nor a compound effect was detectable, in agreement with field studies of the same forest type, so the additive specification of the parent study was the defensible one, and the ease with which clustered seedlings counted as independent observations produced an interaction was the result that applied beyond this landscape.
