# Compound disturbance, additively modelled

*Testing whether beetle mortality and fire severity interact, in the landscape and in the model, Darkwoods Conservation

## Abstract

Provisional, and deliberately outcome-neutral until the interaction test has run. Compound disturbance is routinely
described as an interaction and routinely modelled as a sum. This study separates the two claims over a mountain pine
beetle outbreak and the mixed-severity wildfire that followed it in the southern Selkirk Mountains of British Columbia,
asking first whether the two agents are associated on the ground and second whether their effects on conifer
regeneration multiply or merely add. The first result is negative and concerns the data rather than the forest. The
annual beetle classifications from which every beetle surface in the parent study descends were clipped to the fire
perimeter before any classification was run, so they carry nodata across almost all of the surrounding unburned forest.
The published red-attack surface is confined more tightly still, to the ground where burn severity was classified, and
reports beetle attack in no cell outside it. Read at face value that implies almost no beetle mortality in the forest
around the fire. The provincial Aerial Overview Survey, an independent record that was never clipped, reports beetle
attack outside the perimeter at a rate no lower than inside it, and the small sliver of annual data that does extend
beyond the perimeter agrees with the survey rather than with the published surface. Any burned against unburned contrast
therefore measures the clip. What survives is the gradient within the perimeter, where every severity class is observed
and equally exposed to the clip, and there the corrected grey-stage union, the published red-stage surface and the
independent survey all agree that attack peaks at moderate severity. Conditioned on lodgepole cover and elevation that
peak attenuates by about two fifths and survives, clearing a spatial block bootstrap interval at every block size the
extent can populate. Two further results are negative and both concern extent rather than ecology. Attack at high
severity is never distinguishable from attack at low severity, so the fall that completes the non-monotone shape is not
supported. And host cover cannot be separated from elevation inside a burn this small, the two correlating at 0.52, so
the landscape-scale finding that host governs the pattern can be neither confirmed nor contradicted here. Question A is
accordingly answered narrowly and within the burn, and the interaction test on regeneration is unaffected.
