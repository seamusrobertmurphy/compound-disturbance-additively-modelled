# Venue note

**Date:** 5 August 2026
**Status: SUPERSEDED the same day by `CJFR-GUIDELINES-VERIFIED-2026-08-05.md`.** The author selected
the Canadian Journal of Forest Research. Kept because it records the shortlist and the reasoning that
led there, and because its reading of what the paper had become still governs the framing.

Two things in it are now known to be wrong or incomplete. It treats the CJFR Discussion article type
as the attraction; that type applies only to papers published within CJFR and is unavailable to us,
and it does not matter because CJFR charges nothing for a Research article either. And it records
Forests, Ecosphere and Methods in Ecology and Evolution as live options; all three are gold open
access with no free route and are ruled out by the author's APC-free constraint.

**Purpose:** record what was verified, what could not be, and what the shape of the finished paper implies.

## What the paper turned out to be

This matters more than any journal's word limit, so it goes first. The manuscript is no longer a
compound-disturbance ecology paper with a methods caveat. Its load-bearing result is methodological:
a published point process model's beetle by fire interaction is decisive under the Poisson likelihood
it is fitted with and absent under inference that respects the clustering the same study documents,
with identical coefficients and intervals two to eight times wider. The ecology, a within-burn
severity gradient, is narrow and local. The provenance finding, that the beetle rasters were clipped
to the fire perimeter before classification, is a data result rather than an ecological one.

Two consequences for venue. The paper is a re-analysis of the author's own published work, which
is a specific and slightly awkward shape that not every journal has a home for. And its transferable
content is about inference on clustered point patterns, which is a methods audience rather than a
forest ecology one.

## Not verified, and why

**Both publisher sites returned HTTP 403 to a direct fetch.** `mdpi.com/journal/forests/instructions`
and the ScienceDirect guide for authors for *Forest Ecology and Management* both refused. Everything
in the table above therefore comes from search-result text quoting those pages, not from the pages
themselves. **Confirm every figure in a browser before acting on it.** In particular:

- Forests word and figure limits: not established. MDPI generally does not impose a hard word limit
  on Articles, but that was not confirmed for this journal.
- Whether the 24 June 2026 Feature Paper invitation carries an APC waiver or discount: **not
  established, and it is the single most decision-relevant unknown.** It is in the invitation email,
  which was not read. Read it.
- *Forest Ecology and Management*: nothing was verified. Whether Elsevier offers a Comment or
  Discussion article type there, and whether the journal will consider a re-analysis of a paper it
  published four months earlier, are both unknown.
- Methods in Ecology and Evolution and Remote Sensing of Environment: not reached at all.

## The shape of the decision, not the decision

**Forests, on the standing invitation.** The path of least resistance and the only one with an
existing editorial relationship. The concern is scope rather than quality: MDPI's own description of
a Feature Paper is a substantial original Article, and this is a re-analysis. That is worth raising
with the inviting editor before submission rather than after review, because a mismatch discovered
late costs a cycle. If the invitation carries a waiver, the APC question disappears and this becomes
the obvious first choice.

**A methods venue.** Methods in Ecology and Evolution or a comparable outlet is the better fit for
what the paper actually demonstrates, because the finding generalises to any clustered point pattern
fitted through a Poisson likelihood and the forest ecology is the case study rather than the point.
The cost is that neither the parent study's readership nor its correction reaches that audience.

**Forest Ecology and Management.** The strongest argument for it is that the parent paper is there,
so a correction to the inference reaches the readers who saw the original claim. The strongest
argument against is that a journal is not obliged to publish a critique of something it published
months earlier, and there may be no article type for it. Worth one email to the handling editor
before any formatting work.

## Recommendation on sequence

Do not format for any template yet. The manuscript renders to HTML and Word from one `.qmd` and the
Elsevier extension is already vendored, so template work is cheap and late. Resolve two things first:
read the Feature Paper invitation for its APC terms and its scope language, and ask the *Forest
Ecology and Management* editor whether a re-analysis of a paper they published is admissible and
under what article type. Both are emails. Neither costs a day.
