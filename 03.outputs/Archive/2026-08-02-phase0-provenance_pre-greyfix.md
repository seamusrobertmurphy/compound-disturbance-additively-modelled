# Phase 0 result: the beetle red-stage surface is masked to the burn

**2 August 2026.** Full derivation is in `01.manuscript/compound-disturbance-additively-modelled.qmd`
§3, in live chunks. Nothing here is quoted from a script. This note is a pointer and a summary, not
a source.

## The test

The task request's §7 Phase 0 asked whether the beetle red-stage raster is confined to the fire
perimeter, and whether that would manufacture the association its §4 reported. Three checks:

1. Red attack cross-tabulated against the fire perimeter at native alignment.
2. The distance from the perimeter at which attacked cells stop, against the distance the raster
   itself extends.
3. The local classified surface against the provincial Aerial Overview Survey, class by class.

## The result

| | |
|---|---|
| Red-attack cells inside the perimeter | 493 |
| Red-attack cells outside the perimeter | 0 |
| Raster cells outside the perimeter | 3,429, being 38.5 per cent of the raster |
| Furthest an attacked cell reaches beyond the perimeter | 0 m |
| Distance the raster extends beyond the perimeter | 763 m |
| Survey attack rate on that outside ground | 40.8 per cent |
| Survey attack rate inside the perimeter but unclassified | 52.0 per cent, the highest of any class |

Inside the classified burn the two records agree in the ordinary way, the local surface running at
0.26 to 0.51 of the survey rate. Outside it the local surface returns exactly zero and the survey
returns its two highest rates. The classification was run only where burn severity had been
classified.

## What it costs and what it leaves

Any comparison crossing the classified burn boundary measures the mask, so question A cannot be
asked as burned against unburned. Comparisons inside the mask are unaffected, because all three
severity classes sit within it. There, red attack runs 6.3 per cent at low severity, 20.6 at
moderate and 12.1 at high, and the independent survey traces the same non-monotone shape at 24.3,
40.5 and 33.0 per cent.

Question B is untouched: the seedling point pattern lies wholly inside the burn.

## The caveat that governs Phase 1

Moderate severity occupies the highest mean elevation of the three burned classes at 1,448 m, and
the companion study finds beetle attack peaking near 1,430 m for reasons unrelated to fire. The
agreement between the two records may be agreement about elevation. Condition on host and elevation
before believing the gradient.
